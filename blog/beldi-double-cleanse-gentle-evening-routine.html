A Double-Cleanse Routine with Beldi Black Soap: A Gentle Evening Wash

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 9 November 2026

What double cleansing means

Double cleansing sounds like a complicated salon treatment, but it is a simple idea: wash twice, gently, instead of scrubbing hard once. The first wash lifts away the day — dust, sweat, sunscreen and the light oil that builds up on the skin — and the second wash cleans the skin itself. Each wash is short and soft, and together they clean more thoroughly than one long, harsh wash ever could.

The idea comes from the same thinking behind the traditional hammam wash, where the skin is softened with warm water and a thin layer of Beldi soap before anything else happens. Nothing is forced. The soap does the work, the hands stay gentle, and the skin is rinsed clean twice. An evening double cleanse brings that same calm rhythm into a normal day at home, and it takes only a few extra minutes.

Why evenings are different from mornings

Evening skin has had a full day. It has collected dust from the road, sweat from the heat, and whatever was put on it in the morning. A single quick wash in the evening often leaves a thin film behind, and that film is what makes skin feel dull or slightly sticky the next morning. The evening wash has more to remove, so it deserves a little more care than the morning one.

That is also why the morning routine can stay short. Our morning routine with Beldi black soap is built around one quick, gentle wash, because morning skin is mostly clean already. The evening is the time for the deeper clean, and a double cleanse fits there naturally. Two short washes in the evening, one short wash in the morning, and the skin stays clean without ever being scrubbed raw.

The first wash: lifting away the day

Start with lukewarm water. Splash the face, neck and any other skin you plan to wash until it is fully wet, then take a small amount of Beldi black soap — about the size of a pea for the face, a little more for a wider area — and work it into a soft lather in your hands. Spread the lather over the skin with slow, upward circles and let it sit for about thirty seconds.

This first wash is not meant to be perfect. Its job is to loosen and lift the day away, so do not press hard or go over the same spot again and again. Rinse with lukewarm water and pat the skin lightly. You will notice the rinse water runs a little cloudy on dusty days, and that is the first wash doing exactly what it should. The skin should feel cleaner already, but not tight.

The second wash: a soft, thorough clean

Now wash a second time, with an even smaller amount of soap. Because the first wash removed the surface layer, the second wash reaches the skin itself, and it needs very little soap to do it. Use the same slow circles, spend a little extra time on the forehead, nose and chin where oil gathers, and keep the soap away from the eyes. Thirty seconds is enough.

Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water until the skin feels smooth and clean, not slippery. If any soap is left behind it can dry the skin out, so take the time to rinse well, especially around the hairline and jaw. Pat dry with a clean, soft towel — never rub — and look at the skin: it should feel calm and soft, not red or tight. That is the sign the double cleanse worked.

After the wash: moisturiser on damp skin

Washing always takes a little moisture with it, even when the soap is gentle, so the step after the double cleanse matters as much as the wash itself. While the skin is still slightly damp, smooth on a light, plain moisturiser. Damp skin holds moisture better than dry skin, and a thin layer is enough. This one step is what keeps the evening routine from leaving the skin feeling dry overnight.

If your skin is on the oilier side, keep the moisturiser light and use a small amount; if it is dry, a slightly richer layer on the cheeks is fine. The point is the same either way: the wash cleans, the moisturiser protects, and the two work together. Mistakes to avoid with a double cleanse

The most common mistake is using too much soap in both washes. A double cleanse is two light washes, not two heavy ones, and a thick layer of soap is harder to rinse off and can leave the skin feeling dry. Keep each amount small — a pea-sized amount for the face is enough — and let the second wash be lighter than the first. The second mistake is hot water. It feels relaxing, but it dries the skin out and makes the whole routine harsher than it needs to be.

The third mistake is doing the double cleanse twice a day. Once, in the evening, is enough; the morning wash should stay a single gentle wash. And the fourth is scrubbing between washes with a rough cloth or brush. The soap does the cleaning, and hard scrubbing only leaves redness behind. Keep the evening double cleanse simple and regular, and it becomes a quiet habit: two soft washes, a rinse, a light moisturiser, and skin that feels clean and calm before bed.
