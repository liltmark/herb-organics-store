A Hammam-Style Routine at Home with Beldi Black Soap: Steam, Lather and Rinse

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 18 November 2026

What a hammam-style routine is

A hammam is a traditional steam bath, and its routine is simple at heart: warm steam softens the skin, a rich lather cleans it gently, and a cool rinse finishes the wash. The routine has been used for generations in homes across North Africa and the Middle East, and it needs no special room, only a warm bathroom, a little time and a gentle soap.

The steam does the slow work first, opening the pores and loosening the day from the skin, and the soap then lifts away sweat and dust with a soft lather. Our guide to Beldi black soap ingredients explains what is inside the bar and why it suits this slow, warm routine so well.

Step one: steam the skin first

Start by making the bathroom warm and steamy. Run the shower hot for a few minutes with the door closed, or fill the basin with hot water and let the steam rise. Sit in the warmth for five to ten minutes and let the steam settle on the skin. The skin will feel soft and slightly damp, and that softness is the whole point of the steam.

Do not make the steam too hot, since very hot steam leaves the skin red and uncomfortable. The air should feel warm and close, like a summer evening after rain, not like a boiling pot. If the skin feels tight or the face feels flushed, open the door for a minute and let cooler air in. Our cooling wash routine for hot, humid days shows how to keep a warm wash comfortable when the weather is already hot.

Step two: lather the soap slowly

Wet the soap bar and work it between wet hands until a thick, dark lather forms. Spread the lather over the body in slow, even strokes, starting at the shoulders and working down the arms, chest, back and legs. Use the palms rather than a rough cloth, and let the lather sit on the skin for a minute before rinsing, so the wash has time to work.

The lather should feel soft and smooth, not gritty or dry. If the lather thins out, wet the hands again and work up a fresh layer instead of pressing harder on the skin. A second light layer of lather is better than a hard scrub with the first one. Our gentle exfoliation routine with Beldi black soap explains how often to add a light exfoliating step to a routine like this one.

Step three: rinse with cool water

Rinse the lather away with cool to lukewarm water, starting at the shoulders and letting the water run down the body. The cool rinse is the part of the routine that wakes the skin up after the warm steam, and it leaves the skin feeling fresh and firm instead of limp and overheated. Rinse until no trace of lather remains, since leftover soap leaves the skin feeling dry later.

Take your time with the rinse and check the spots that are easy to miss, like the back of the neck, behind the knees and between the toes. A full rinse matters more than a fast one, and cool water closes the wash in a gentle way. How often to do a hammam-style wash

Once a week is enough for most people, and it works well as a slow weekend wash. A daily hammam-style routine would be too much steam and too much time, and the skin does not need a long steam bath every day. Keep the weekday washes short and simple, and save the full steam, lather and rinse routine for a day when there is time to enjoy it.

On the days between hammam washes, a normal short wash with the same soap keeps the skin clean without overdoing it. The skin stays soft because the routine is steady, not because it is long every day. If the skin feels dry after a hammam wash, shorten the steam step next time instead of skipping the routine. Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is making the steam too hot or staying in it too long, which leaves the skin red and tired instead of soft. The second is scrubbing hard while the skin is soft from the steam, since soft skin marks easily and a hard scrub turns a gentle routine into a rough one. The third is skipping the cool rinse and stepping out still warm and soapy, which leaves the skin feeling sticky.

The fourth mistake is using a rough cloth or brush on skin that is already soft from the steam, since the soap and the palms are enough. And the fifth is treating the hammam wash as a daily job, which turns a calm weekly ritual into a chore. Steam gently, lather slowly, rinse cool, and keep the routine the same for a few weeks before judging it.
