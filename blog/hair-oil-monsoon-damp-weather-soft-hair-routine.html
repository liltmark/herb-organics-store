Hair Oil in the Monsoon: Keeping Hair Soft in Damp, Humid Weather

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 16 November 2026

Why monsoon weather changes how hair behaves

Monsoon air is heavy with moisture, and hair drinks that moisture in all day. The strands swell, the ends lift, and hair that was smooth in dry weather turns frizzy and hard to manage. Rain adds to the trouble, since wet hair that dries slowly in humid air stays damp for hours and feels limp and sticky.

A little oil helps because it gives the hair a light shield against the damp air. The oil sits on the surface of the strands and slows down how much moisture the hair takes in, so the hair stays softer and smoother between washes. Our monsoon wash routine with herbal shampoo follows the same idea, and it shows how a gentle wash clears the damp-weather build-up without drying the hair out.

How much oil to use in damp weather

Less is more in the monsoon. A few drops warmed between the palms is enough for the lengths, and a little more only if the hair is long or very dry. Heavy oiling in humid weather leaves the hair looking greasy, since the damp air already weighs the strands down, and a light layer is all the hair needs.

Start with three or four drops and add one more only if the ends still feel rough. Spread the oil over the lengths first, from the middle of the hair down to the ends, and keep it away from the roots on the day you oil. Our guide to how much hair oil different hair types need helps you find the right amount for your own hair.

The monsoon oiling routine, step by step

Oil the hair on dry hair, a few hours before washing. Pour a few drops into the palm, rub the palms together to warm the oil, then smooth it over the lengths with slow strokes. Work from the middle of the hair down to the ends, and take care with the ends, since they dry out first in damp weather.

Leave the oil in for one or two hours, then wash it out with a gentle herbal shampoo. A longer oiling does not help more in the monsoon, since the damp air already keeps the hair soft, and leaving heavy oil in overnight can leave the scalp feeling sticky. Our light leave-in routine for flyaways and frizz shows how a single drop after washing smooths the strands that lift in humid air.

Washing the oil out in humid weather

Washing oil out in the monsoon takes a little patience, since humid air slows the rinse and the hair stays wet longer. Wet the hair well with lukewarm water, then work a small amount of shampoo into the scalp and let the lather carry the oil down the lengths. Rinse twice if the hair still feels oily after the first rinse.

Dry the hair gently with a towel and let it air dry when you can, since a dryer in humid air only pushes damp heat into the strands. If the hair must be tied up, wait until it is mostly dry, because tying wet hair leaves it limp and hard to style. Our weekly hair oiling routine shows how to fit oiling into a regular week, and the same short routine works well through the rainy season.

Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is oiling heavily every day, which leaves the hair greasy in humid air and makes it harder to wash out. The second is oiling the roots on a damp day, which leaves the scalp feeling sticky. The third is going out with wet, oiled hair in the rain, since rain water mixes with the oil and leaves the hair limp.

The fourth mistake is skipping the wash after oiling, since oil left in the hair for days gathers dust and makes the scalp itchy. And the fifth is drying the hair by rubbing it hard with a towel, which roughs up the strands and adds to the frizz. Oil lightly, wash well, dry gently, and keep the routine the same for a few weeks before judging it. Our winter protection routine with hair oil shows the other side of the year, when cold wind and dry air call for a richer layer.

Between washes: keeping hair manageable

Between washes, a single drop of oil on the palms, smoothed over the ends, is enough to calm the frizz that humid air brings. Do not oil the whole head again between washes, since the hair already holds moisture from the air, and a fresh layer only adds weight. A light touch on the ends keeps the hair looking tidy without a full routine.

A loose braid or a soft bun also helps on the dampest days, since it keeps the hair from tangling in the wind and rain. Cover the hair with a light scarf on a rainy day, and let it dry fully once you are indoors. Our scalp massage technique with hair oil is a calm evening habit for the monsoon too, and a slow massage with a few drops helps the scalp feel fresh after a damp day.
