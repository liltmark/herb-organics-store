Hair Oil for Colour Treated Hair: A Gentle Care Routine

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 26 November 2026

Hair oil for colour treated hair: why coloured hair needs gentle care

Colouring opens the hair surface to place the colour inside, and that opening leaves the hair drier and more fragile than before. Hair oil for colour treated hair fits that need well, because a light oil smooths the surface, holds moisture in, and keeps the lengths soft between washes. Colour treated hair care starts with gentle handling, and oiling is one of the gentlest habits in the routine.

Coloured hair loses its colour slowly with every wash, and rough handling speeds that loss up. Hot water, hard scrubbing and strong shampoos all push the colour out faster, while gentle oiling and a calm wash slow the fade down. No oil can stop the colour fading completely, and no honest routine should claim that it can, but a gentle routine keeps the colour looking fresh for longer, and that is what the oiling is for.

The weekly oiling routine for coloured hair

Oil once a week to start, on the evening before a wash day, and keep the amount small until the hair shows what it needs. Warm a few drops between the palms, smooth them over the lengths first, then work the rest lightly over the scalp. The lengths need the most oil, because they are the oldest and driest part of the hair, and the colour sits in them the longest. Leave the oil for at least half an hour, or overnight if the hair feels very dry. The weekly hair oiling routine shows the full order: oil, rest and wash.

Use less oil than feels necessary in the first weeks, because heavy oiling makes the wash harder, and a harder wash fades the colour faster. A few drops, spread well, cover the whole length when the smoothing is slow. The oil should feel like a thin layer, not a coating, and the hair should still move freely. Gentle hair oiling starts with a light hand, and the amount can grow only when the hair asks for more.

Washing out the oil without fading the colour

Wash the oil out the next morning with a gentle herbal shampoo, and use lukewarm water, never hot, since hot water opens the hair surface and lets the colour escape. One wash is usually enough if the oiling was light, and a second wash is fine if the hair still feels coated. Scrubbing the scalp hard to remove the oil defeats the purpose, because rough washing fades the colour and breaks the very hair the oiling protected. Rinse until the water runs clear, then finish with a final rinse of cool water, which helps the hair surface settle and holds the colour in. Pat the hair dry with a soft towel instead of rubbing it, since wet coloured hair is at its weakest. Comb only when the hair is mostly dry, starting from the ends and working upward, with a wide-toothed comb.

Sun, heat and water: protecting coloured hair between washes

Strong sun fades coloured hair faster than almost anything else, because the light breaks the colour down a little every day. Cover the hair with a scarf or a hat in strong sun, and keep long hours in the sun to a minimum. Chlorine and salt water also pull at the colour, so wet the hair with plain water before swimming, and wash it gently as soon as the swim is over.

Heat styling fades the colour too, so keep the iron and the dryer on a low setting, and use them less often when the colour is fresh. A light layer of oil on the lengths before styling gives the hair a little protection, but it does not make heat safe, and the best protection is simply less heat. Hair oil as pre-styling heat protection shows how to oil the lengths before styling.

How much oil, and how often

Most coloured hair does well with one light oiling a week, and the amount depends on the length: short hair needs only a few drops, while long hair needs a little more for the ends. Judge by how the hair feels the next morning: if it feels soft and the wash is easy, the amount was right, and if it feels heavy and coated, use less next time. The goal is softness, not shine from a thick layer.

In dry winter air, the hair may ask for a second light oiling midweek, and in humid weather one weekly oiling is usually enough. Listen to the hair, and change the amount slowly, since the colour and the dryness change with the season. Protect hair colour by keeping the routine steady, and let the oil work quietly in the background.

Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is oiling heavily and then scrubbing hard to wash it out, which fades the colour faster than the oiling helped. The second is washing coloured hair with hot water, which opens the surface and lets the colour escape. The third is colouring the hair again too soon, before the last colour has settled, since repeated colouring dries the hair out and makes it fragile.

The fourth mistake is skipping the oiling altogether, because coloured hair without oil turns dry and rough, and rough hair loses its colour faster. The fifth is expecting the oil to fix damage, since oil protects and softens, and it cannot repair hair that is already broken. A gentle care routine keeps coloured hair soft and the colour fresh, and that is the honest work the oil does.
