Hair Oil Before Swimming: Protecting Hair from Chlorine and Salt Water

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 7 November 2026

A swim is one of the best feelings of summer — until you get out of the pool and your hair feels like straw. Chlorine and salt water both pull moisture out of the hair, and they do it fast. Chlorine strips the hair's natural oils and roughs up its surface, which is why pool hair feels dry and tangles easily. Salt water does something similar: the salt crystals left behind as the hair dries draw moisture out and leave the hair stiff and brittle. The damage is not permanent, but it builds up over a summer of regular swimming. The fix is simple and older than any salon product: oil the hair before you get in the water. A light coat of hair oil gives the water something to work through before it reaches the hair itself, and it makes the after-swim wash far easier.

How chlorine and salt water damage hair

Hair is not waterproof. Each strand soaks up water like a sponge, and when that water is full of chlorine or salt, the chemicals and minerals come along with it. Chlorine is a strong cleaner — that is its job in a pool — and it strips away the natural oils that keep hair soft. Without those oils, the surface of the hair roughens, the strands catch on each other, and combing after a swim becomes a battle. Over a whole summer, regular swimmers often notice their hair getting lighter in colour and more likely to snap.

Salt water is gentler than chlorine but still drying. The salt itself does not damage the hair the way chlorine does, but as the water evaporates the salt crystals left on the hair pull moisture out of the strand. Hair that dries in the sun after a sea swim gets the worst of it, because the sun adds its own drying effect on top. The result is the same: stiff, tangled, thirsty hair. The ends suffer most, because they are the oldest and driest part of the hair already. Our guide to protecting split ends explains why the ends need the most care, and swimming is one of the fastest ways to make them worse.

The pre-swim oiling routine

Oil the hair about twenty to thirty minutes before you swim. That gives the oil time to settle into the hair instead of sitting on the surface where the water will wash it straight off. Take a small amount of hair oil — a coin-sized pool in your palm for shoulder-length hair, a little more for longer hair — and warm it between your hands. Smooth it over the lengths and the ends first, because those are the parts that dry out fastest. Then work what is left through the mid-lengths. Keep the oil off the scalp, where it only makes the after-swim wash harder.

If your hair is long, braid it loosely or tie it in a low bun after oiling. Loose hair in a pool tangles around itself and soaks up far more water than hair that is gathered up. A swim cap helps too, and it works even better over oiled hair. For a beach swim, the same routine applies.

After the swim: washing the oil and chlorine out

Rinse the hair with fresh water as soon as you can after swimming. A quick rinse under a shower or even a bottle of fresh water removes most of the chlorine and salt before it dries into the hair. Then wash with a gentle herbal shampoo, focusing on the scalp and letting the lather travel down the lengths.

After washing, a small amount of oil on the ends only — never the scalp — replaces the moisture the swim took away. Our guide to washing hair after oiling walks through the same rinse-and-wash order, and it works just as well for a post-swim wash as for a regular oiling day. If you swim several times a week, keep the routine light: a little oil before each swim and a gentle wash after.

Mistakes swimmers make with their hair

The biggest mistake is getting into the pool with completely dry hair. Dry hair soaks up pool water like a sponge, and the chlorine goes straight in with it. Wetting the hair with fresh water before you swim fills the strands with clean water first, so there is less room for chlorinated water to soak in. Wet hair, then oil, then swim — in that order — and the hair takes in far less chlorine.

The second mistake is leaving pool water to dry in the hair under the sun. The water evaporates and the chlorine and salt stay behind. Rinsing with fresh water before you sit down in the sun takes two minutes and saves the hair a lot of trouble. And the third mistake is combing wet, tangled swim hair with a fine brush. After a swim, detangle gently with your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, starting at the ends. Our guide on hair oil for curly and wavy hair shows the same gentle detangling method, and it matters even more after chlorine has roughened the strands.

A swim-day routine that works

Put it together and a swim day looks like this: wet the hair with fresh water, smooth a light coat of hair oil over the lengths and ends, tie the hair up loosely, swim, rinse with fresh water straight after, wash gently with a herbal shampoo, and finish with a touch of oil on the ends. It takes five minutes before the swim and five after, and it keeps a summer of swimming from turning into a season of dry, snapping hair.
