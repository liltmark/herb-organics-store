Hair Oil Before Washing: A Gentle Detangling Routine for Tangly Hair

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 15 November 2026

Why oil helps before washing

Tangly hair knots worst when it is wet, and wet hair is also at its weakest. Brushing or combing through knots after a wash pulls at the hair and breaks the ends, which only makes the next wash tanglier. A little oil before washing changes that order: the oil coats the lengths, lets the strands slide past each other, and makes the knots easier to loosen before water ever touches the hair.

Oil also softens the hair, so the wash itself is gentler. Hair that has been lightly oiled rinses clean with less scrubbing, and it combs more easily while it dries. Our gentle detangling wash for tangly hair covers the washing half of this routine, and oiling first makes that wash noticeably easier.

How much oil to use

Start small. A few drops warmed between the palms is enough for short hair, and a small spoonful is enough for long hair. The oil belongs on the lengths and the ends, where the tangles form, not on the scalp, since oil on the scalp makes the wash harder to rinse out.

Add more only if the hair still feels dry and rough after the first pass. A little goes a long way, and you can always add a second small amount if the ends still feel dry after the first pass. Too much oil leaves the hair heavy and greasy, and it takes two washes to rinse out, which defeats the purpose. Our guide to how much hair oil each hair type needs shows the right amount for fine, thick, and curly hair, and the same small start works here.

The detangling routine, step by step

Warm the oil between your palms and smooth it over the lengths of dry hair, from the ears down to the ends. Leave it for ten to fifteen minutes so it can soften the strands. Then, with your fingers, gently pull the larger knots apart, starting at the ends and working up a little at a time.

Once the fingers move freely, take a wide-tooth comb and work through the hair in the same order, ends first, then upwards. Never start at the roots, since that pushes every knot down into a tighter tangle. Washing the oil out properly

Wet the hair with lukewarm water and wash with a small amount of herbal shampoo, working the lather through the lengths. Rinse well, and wash a second time only if the hair still feels oily after the first rinse. Most of the time one careful wash is enough when the oiling was light.

If the water in your area is hard, the rinse matters even more, since mineral residue can leave the hair feeling rough and dull. Take an extra minute with the rinse, letting the water run through the lengths until it feels clean and smooth, and the oil will wash out fully without a second round of shampoo. The hair should feel soft and move freely, with no heavy or greasy patches. Our hair wash routine after oiling covers this rinse in full, and it shows why a patient rinse matters more than a second wash.

Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is oiling the scalp heavily, which makes the wash hard to rinse and leaves the hair limp. The second is combing from the roots down through dry knots, which breaks the ends. The third is leaving the oil on for hours when ten to fifteen minutes is enough for detangling.

The fourth mistake is using a fine comb on tangled hair, which snags and pulls instead of sliding through. And the fifth is skipping the detangling step and hoping the wash will sort the knots out, since water only tightens them. Oil lightly, detangle patiently, wash once, and tangly hair becomes far easier to manage.

How often to oil before washing

Once or twice a week is enough for most hair. Oiling before every wash can leave the hair heavy, while oiling too rarely lets the tangles build up again. A good rhythm is to oil on the wash days when the hair feels driest or knottiest, and to skip it on the days when the hair already combs easily.

Watch how the hair behaves after the wash. If it feels soft and combs smoothly, the amount and the timing are right. If it still feels rough, leave the oil on a few minutes longer next time, and if it feels heavy, use less oil or oil less often.
