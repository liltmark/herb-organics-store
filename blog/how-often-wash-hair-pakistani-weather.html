How Often Should You Wash Your Hair? A Gentle Routine for Pakistani Weather

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 27 October 2026

Ask five people how often you should wash your hair and you will get five different answers. Every day, twice a week, only on Sundays: everyone is sure their own routine is the right one. The honest answer is less satisfying but far more useful — it depends. It depends on your hair type, on how much you sweat, on whether you work outdoors, and, in Pakistan more than most places, on the season. A routine that feels perfect in January can leave your hair limp and flat in July, and a summer routine can leave it dry and rough in December. The trick is not to find one fixed rule, but to learn how to adjust.

There is no single correct number

Hair does not come with a washing schedule printed on it. Some scalps produce more natural oil than others, fine hair shows oil sooner because there is less hair to spread it over, and thick or curly hair can go longer between washes without looking greasy. Lifestyle matters just as much. A person who works in an air-conditioned office will not need to wash as often as someone who spends the day on a motorbike in traffic, or a student walking to classes in the heat. Instead of copying someone else's schedule, start with a sensible middle point — washing every second or third day — and then adjust up or down based on how your own hair looks and feels.

That middle point works because it balances two things. Wash too often with a harsh shampoo, and you strip away the natural oils that keep hair feeling soft, leaving it dry and harder to manage. Wash too rarely, and oil, sweat and dust build up, leaving hair flat, heavy and uncomfortable. A gentle shampoo makes the balance much easier to hold, because it cleans without leaving the hair feeling stripped. That is why the shampoo you use matters almost as much as how often you use it.

Summer: heat, sweat and humidity

Pakistani summers are the hardest season for hair. Heat makes you sweat, humidity makes hair fall flat, and dust from the road settles on everything. In June, July and August, most people genuinely need to wash more often — every second day is normal, and daily washing is understandable if you have been out in the heat all day, especially if your hair is fine or short. The key in summer is to keep each wash gentle. Use lukewarm water rather than very hot water, use only as much shampoo as you need for one good lather on the scalp, and let the lather run down the lengths rather than scrubbing the ends hard.

Winter: dry air and hot water

Winter brings the opposite problem. The air is drier, indoor heating dries things further, and many of us start taking much hotter showers to warm up. Hot water feels wonderful on a cold morning, but it also leaves hair feeling rougher and drier, and it can leave the scalp feeling tight. In winter, most people can wash less often — every third or fourth day suits many hair types — and should turn the water temperature down a notch, even if the shower itself stays warm.

Monsoon, dust and in-between seasons

The monsoon and the dusty shoulder seasons deserve a mention of their own, because they break every neat seasonal rule. A sudden downpour can leave hair damp and full of road dust, and a dusty wind can do the same on a dry day. After exposure like that, an extra wash is simply practical, even if it falls outside your usual schedule. Do not feel you have broken a rule by washing an extra time; routines are meant to serve real life, not the other way round. Similarly, a week spent mostly indoors during heavy rain may mean you can comfortably stretch a day longer between washes.

How to tell if you are washing too much, or too little

Your hair will tell you, if you listen to it rather than to a fixed schedule. Signs you may be washing too often include hair that feels dry and rough even straight after washing, ends that tangle easily, and a scalp that feels tight or uncomfortable. Signs you may be washing too rarely include hair that looks flat and heavy soon after washing, a scalp that feels oily or uncomfortable, and an itchy feeling that comes from build-up rather than dryness. The two sets of signs can feel similar at first, which is why it helps to change only one thing at a time — wash one day less often for two weeks, or use a little less shampoo — and see what changes.

A gentle wash, whatever your schedule

However often you wash, the method stays the same. Wet the hair thoroughly, work a modest amount of NABAT Herbal & Organic Shampoo into the scalp with your fingertips, and let the lather run down the lengths. Rinse well, and if the hair still feels heavy, a second light lather on the scalp is better than one very heavy lather. Finish with lukewarm water, pat dry gently, and let the hair air-dry when you can. If you oil your hair as part of your routine, our weekly hair oiling routine pairs naturally with this washing method: oil the evening before, wash gently the next morning, and you have a complete routine that works in every season.

The best washing routine is not the strictest one. It is the one you can keep up through summer heat, winter dryness and dusty days in between, without your hair feeling punished for it. Start in the middle, adjust with the seasons, and let how your hair feels guide you. That is a routine worth keeping.
