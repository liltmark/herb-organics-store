Herbal Ingredients for Winter Skin: A Gentle Guide to Amla, Reetha and Shikakai

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 14 November 2026

Why winter skin needs different care

Winter brings cold air outside and dry, heated air inside, and skin feels the change quickly. It turns dry, tight, and rough, and the hands, lips, and shins often show it first. A wash routine that feels fine in summer can leave winter skin stripped and uncomfortable, so the ingredients in the wash matter more in the cold months.

Three herbal ingredients have been trusted in our homes for generations: amla, reetha, and shikakai. Each one does a different job, and together they make a gentle winter routine that cleans without stripping the skin. Our guide to amla, reetha and shikakai for hair explains how the same three ingredients care for the hair, and this guide looks at what they do for winter skin.

Amla: gentle care for dry skin

Amla is rich in vitamin C and has been used in home skin care for a long time. In winter, it helps skin that feels dull and tired, since it supports the skin gently without irritating it. Amla is usually used as a powder mixed with water or rose water, and applied as a light mask or a gentle wash.

For winter skin, a thin amla paste left on the skin for a few minutes and then rinsed off with lukewarm water can leave the skin feeling fresh and soft. Do not leave it on for long, and do not use it on broken or very sensitive skin. A little goes a long way, and once a week is enough.

Reetha: a soft, natural wash

Reetha, also called soapnut, is a natural cleanser that makes a soft, mild lather. It cleans the skin without the harsh feeling that strong soaps can leave, and it suits winter skin well because it does not strip the skin dry. The lather is light and rinses off easily, which matters when the skin is already dry from the cold.

Reetha can be used as a gentle wash for the hands and the body in winter, especially for skin that reacts to strong soaps. Soak the dried fruit in warm water, use the water as a wash, and rinse well. Our guide to choosing a herbal soap for sensitive skin explains how to test any new wash on a small area first, and the same care applies to reetha.

Shikakai: gentle cleansing without stripping

Shikakai is a mild, natural cleanser that has been used for skin and hair care for generations. It cleans gently and leaves the skin feeling soft rather than tight, which is exactly what winter skin needs. Like reetha, it is usually used as a powder mixed with water, or as a soak.

In winter, shikakai works well as a gentle wash for the body, especially for skin that feels dry after a hot shower. Use a small amount, keep the wash short, and rinse with lukewarm water. Follow with a plain moisturiser while the skin is still slightly damp, so the moisture stays in.

Using the three together in winter

Amla, reetha, and shikakai work well together because each one covers a different need: amla for gentle care, reetha for a soft wash, and shikakai for mild cleansing. A simple winter mix uses a little of each powder, mixed with lukewarm water into a thin paste, and used as a gentle wash once or twice a week.

Do not mix them in large amounts or use the mix every day. Winter skin needs gentle care, not constant treatment, and a weekly wash with these ingredients is enough. Between these washes, keep the routine simple: a gentle daily wash and a plain moisturiser.

A simple weekly winter routine

Start the week with the gentle herbal wash, using the three-ingredient mix on the body and rinsing well with lukewarm water. Moisturise while the skin is still slightly damp, and keep the rest of the week simple: a short daily wash with a gentle soap, such as Beldi black soap, and a plain moisturiser every evening.

Pay extra attention to the areas that dry out fastest in winter, such as the hands, the lips, and the shins. Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is using very hot water in winter, which strips the skin and leaves it drier than before. The second is scrubbing winter skin hard to remove dryness, which only irritates it. The third is skipping moisturiser, because the skin feels fine for an hour after the wash and then turns dry and tight by night.

The fourth mistake is using strong, perfumed products on winter skin, which can sting and irritate it. And the fifth is trying all three ingredients at full strength on the first day, instead of starting small and testing on one area. Wash gently, moisturise well, and let these three old ingredients do their quiet work.
