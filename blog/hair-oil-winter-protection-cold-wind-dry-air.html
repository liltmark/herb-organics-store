Protecting Hair in Winter with Hair Oil: Cold, Wind and Dry Air Care

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 4 November 2026

Winter is hard on hair in ways that are easy to miss until the damage shows. Cold air outside, dry heated air inside, biting wind on the way to work, and wool caps and scarves rubbing the same strands every day — together they pull moisture out of the hair, rough up the surface, and leave lengths feeling brittle, dull, and full of static. Hair does not repair itself the way skin does, so winter care is mostly about protection: keeping moisture in, reducing friction, and giving the hair a regular dose of the oil it loses to the season.

What winter actually does to your hair

Cold air holds very little moisture, so hair exposed to it loses water to the air, and the effect is worse when you move between cold outdoors and warm, dry indoor heating several times a day. Wind adds a second problem: it tangles the lengths, rubs strands against each other and against collars and scarves, and that friction roughens the hair surface, which is why winter hair often looks dull and feels rough even when it is clean. Static is the visible sign of the same dryness. Add hot showers, which strip the scalp and lengths further, and hats worn for hours, which flatten the roots, and you have a season that quietly wears hair down week by week.

A weekly winter oiling routine

Once a week through winter, give your hair a proper oiling session. Warm a small amount of oil between your palms, work it into the scalp with your fingertips, and then smooth what is left down the lengths, paying particular attention to the ends, which are the oldest and driest part of the hair. Leave it for at least an hour, or overnight if that suits your schedule, with the hair loosely covered so the oil stays on the hair rather than on your pillow. Wash it out with lukewarm water and a gentle shampoo, rinsing thoroughly. If you are unsure how much oil your hair type needs, our guide on how much oil to use for different hair types is a useful starting point, and our weekly hair oiling routine covers the basic rhythm in more detail. In deep winter, when the air is at its driest, a second, lighter mid-week oiling of the ends alone can help without making the roots heavy.

Cold, wind and dry air: day-by-day protection

Between oiling sessions, small daily habits protect the hair from the weather. On windy days, a loose braid or a low bun keeps the lengths from whipping around and tangling, which is the single easiest way to cut winter breakage. A smooth scarf or a silk-lined cap reduces friction far better than rough wool worn directly against the hair, and tucking the ends inside a coat or scarf on a bitter day keeps the most fragile part of the hair out of the wind entirely. Indoors, dry heated air is the quiet culprit, so if your home feels very dry, a bowl of water near the heater or a damp towel in the room adds a little moisture back to the air. And when you come in from the cold, let the hair warm up naturally rather than blasting it with a hot dryer straight away. If your ends are already dry and rough, our dry ends care routine explains how to seal and protect them.

Washing and drying in winter

Winter washing needs a gentler hand. Keep the water lukewarm, never hot, because hot water strips the scalp and leaves the lengths even drier, and wash a little less often if your scalp allows it, since every wash removes some of the oil the hair needs in cold weather. After washing, squeeze the water out gently with a soft towel instead of rubbing vigorously, which roughens wet hair when it is at its most fragile. Let the hair air-dry as far as possible, and if you must use a dryer, keep it on a low or cool setting and hold it at a distance. On wash days, a tiny amount of oil smoothed over just the ends while the hair is still slightly damp seals in moisture and tames static without weighing the roots down. If your hair is curly or wavy, our routine for curly and wavy hair has more on keeping definition through dry weather.

Winter mistakes that undo the good work

A few common winter habits undo an otherwise good routine. The first is going out with wet hair in cold air, which leaves the strands stiff and more likely to snap, so always let the hair dry before stepping out on a cold morning. The second is over-washing in an attempt to control static or a hat-flattened style, which strips the very moisture the hair needs. The third is skipping oil entirely because the hair looks flat under a cap, when a light ends-only oiling would have protected the lengths without touching the roots. And the fourth is ignoring the ends until spring, by which point split and frayed ends have travelled further up the hair and need cutting rather than care. Our protective routine for split ends and breakage covers what to do when the ends are already struggling.

A routine you can actually keep

Put together, winter hair protection is simple: one proper oiling session a week, a lighter ends-only oiling mid-week in the driest weeks, lukewarm washes, gentle drying, and the lengths kept out of the wind with a loose braid or a smooth scarf. Judge the routine by how the ends feel, not just how the roots look, because the ends show winter damage first. Start the routine as the cold sets in rather than waiting for the damage to appear. Hair that goes into winter protected comes out of it softer, stronger, and needing far less repair in spring.
