Caring for Colour-Treated Hair with a Gentle Herbal Wash: Keep Colour Looking Fresh Longer

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 1 November 2026

Colour-treated hair asks for a gentler kind of care than untreated hair. After colouring, the hair often feels drier and rougher to the touch, tangles more easily, and loses its fresh colour sooner than expected, especially at the ends. The wash routine is where most of that fading happens, not in a single dramatic moment but gradually, wash after wash, through hot water, rough handling and washing too often. A gentle herbal wash routine slows that process down. It will not freeze colour in place forever, but it helps colour look fresh for longer and keeps the hair feeling softer and easier to manage between salon visits.

Why colour-treated hair needs gentler washing

Colouring changes the surface of the hair, leaving it more open and more ready to absorb and release moisture than before. That is why coloured hair can feel dry even when it looked healthy before colouring, and why it reacts more strongly to hot water and vigorous washing. Each wash lifts a little colour along with the dirt, and rough handling — piling the hair on top of the head, scrubbing the lengths, or towel-drying aggressively — roughens the surface further, which makes the colour look duller. Gentle washing works with this changed surface instead of against it: lukewarm water, a small amount of shampoo, and attention focused on the scalp rather than the lengths.

Before you wash: detangle first

Always detangle before the water goes on, because wet hair tangles far more easily and breaks more readily when pulled through. Use a wide-tooth comb or your fingers, starting from the ends and working upward in short sections, so knots come out without dragging through the whole length. This one habit makes the rest of the wash easier: the shampoo spreads evenly, rinsing is quicker, and there is far less pulling afterwards. If the ends feel particularly rough, a little of your usual conditioner smoothed over just the ends before washing can help them slide past each other during the wash, though the scalp itself should stay free of conditioner at this stage.

The wash itself: scalp first, lengths second

Wet the hair thoroughly with lukewarm water, never hot, and take a small amount of Nabat Herbal Shampoo, working it into a light lather between damp hands first. Apply it to the scalp with the pads of the fingers, using slow, gentle circles, and let the lather run down the lengths as you rinse rather than scrubbing the lengths directly. The lengths do not need vigorous washing: the rinse-through carries away surface dust and oil without roughing up the coloured surface. Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water until the water runs clear, and repeat only if the scalp still feels unclean after the first wash — one gentle wash is usually enough, and a second wash should be lighter than the first.

How often to wash coloured hair

Stretching the time between washes is one of the simplest ways to help colour look fresh for longer, because every wash, however gentle, lifts a little colour. For most people with colour-treated hair, washing two or three times a week is a comfortable rhythm, with plain water rinses or a loose, covered style on the days between. On non-wash days, keep the hair loosely tied or covered outdoors, since sun and dust both dull colour over time, and rinse with plain lukewarm water after swimming or heavy sweating rather than reaching for shampoo each time. If the scalp feels uncomfortable between washes, a gentle scalp rinse with water alone is kinder to colour than an extra full wash. Conditioning habits that protect colour

After washing, apply your usual conditioner mainly to the mid-lengths and ends, where coloured hair feels driest, and keep it away from the scalp, where it can leave the roots feeling heavy. Leave it on for a minute or two while you finish your shower, then rinse with cool or lukewarm water, which helps the hair feel smoother afterwards. Comb through gently with a wide-tooth comb while the hair is still damp, starting from the ends upward, and let the hair air-dry whenever you can rather than rubbing it hard with a towel. Pat or squeeze gently with a soft towel instead, and save heat styling for occasions rather than every day, since repeated high heat fades colour and dries the ends faster than almost anything else.

Mistakes that fade colour faster

Hot water tops the list: it feels relaxing but opens the hair surface and lets colour wash out faster with every rinse. Daily vigorous washing comes next, especially scrubbing the lengths or piling the hair up and rubbing it together. Rough towel drying, brushing wet hair with a fine brush, and backcombing all roughen the coloured surface and make it look dull sooner. So does washing immediately after colouring before the colour has had time to settle — give it at least a couple of days before the first wash. A simple weekly routine for coloured hair

Put together, the week looks calm and simple: detangle before every wash, wash two or three times a week with lukewarm water and a scalp-focused lather, condition the lengths and ends, and let the hair air-dry when possible. Protect it from strong sun and dust outdoors, rinse rather than shampoo after swimming, and keep heat styling occasional.
