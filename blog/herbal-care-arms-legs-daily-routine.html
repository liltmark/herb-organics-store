Herbal Care for Arms and Legs: A Simple Daily Routine for Soft Skin

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 9 November 2026

Why arms and legs get forgotten

Most skin care attention goes to the face, and most of the rest goes to the hands. Arms and legs are washed quickly in the shower and then forgotten, yet they face the same sun, dust and dry air as the rest of the body — and the shins and forearms often face more of it, since they are uncovered for much of the year. The result is skin that looks dull, feels rough to the touch, and dries out faster than the skin we look after properly.

The good news is that arms and legs are easy to care for. The skin there is tougher than face skin, so it tolerates a normal wash well, and it responds quickly to a simple routine. A gentle wash, a light moisturiser on damp skin, and a little extra care for the rough spots once a week is all it takes. Two minutes a day, done regularly, makes a visible difference within a fortnight.

The daily wash: gentle and lukewarm

Wash arms and legs with lukewarm water and a small amount of Beldi black soap. Work the soap into a soft lather in your hands first, then spread it over the skin with slow, even strokes. There is no need to scrub hard — the soap lifts away sweat and dust on its own, and hard scrubbing only leaves the skin red and dry. Rinse well with lukewarm water until the skin feels clean and smooth.

Hot water is the quiet enemy here. A very hot shower feels relaxing, especially in winter, but it strips the natural oils out of the skin on the arms and legs faster than anywhere else, and the shins are the first place to feel it. Lukewarm water cleans just as well and leaves the skin calm. This is the same gentle approach as our full-body shower routine: warm water, a thin layer of soap, a gentle touch, and no scrubbing.

Moisturiser on damp skin

The most important step comes right after the wash, while the skin is still slightly damp. Smooth a light, plain moisturiser over the arms and legs, using slow strokes from the hands toward the shoulders and from the feet upward. Damp skin holds moisture far better than dry skin, so the same moisturiser works much harder when it goes on straight after the shower.

Do not wait until the skin is fully dry and tight before moisturising — by then the moisture has already left, and the cream sits on top instead of sinking in. A thin, even layer is enough; a thick layer is not better, it just takes longer to sink in and can feel sticky under clothes. If your hands and feet need the same treatment, our hand and foot care routine covers them in detail.

Rough patches: elbows, knees and shins

Elbows, knees and shins are the rough spots on the arms and legs. The skin there is thicker, it bends all day, and it dries out first. A daily wash and moisturiser helps, but these spots benefit from a little extra care once a week: after a warm shower, when the skin is soft, smooth a little extra moisturiser over them and let it sink in before dressing. No scrubbing is needed — the warm water has already softened the skin.

If the elbows and knees feel especially rough, give them a gentle weekly routine of their own. Our dry elbows and knees routine walks through it step by step, and the same gentle thinking applies to the neck and chest, another area that is often forgotten — our neck and chest routine covers that skin too. Treat the rough spots kindly and regularly, and they soften over a few weeks.

Sun, dust and the seasons

Arms and legs spend more time in the sun than any other part of the body, and the sun dries skin out steadily through the year. In summer, sweat and dust add to the problem: the skin feels sticky by evening, and a quick rinse alone does not remove the film that builds up. A gentle wash in the evening, followed by moisturiser on damp skin, clears the day away and lets the skin recover overnight.

Winter brings the opposite problem: cold air, hot showers and dry indoor air pull moisture out of the skin, and the shins are usually the first place to feel tight and itchy. The routine does not need to change — the same lukewarm wash and the same moisturiser on damp skin — but the moisturiser can be a slightly richer layer in the cold months. If you are choosing between soaps for the colder season, our comparison of Beldi soap and regular soap explains what makes a gentle wash different.

A simple routine that sticks

The best routine is the one you actually do every day, so keep it short. In the shower: lukewarm water, a small amount of Beldi soap worked into a lather, slow strokes over the arms and legs, a thorough rinse. Out of the shower: pat the skin lightly with a towel, then moisturise while it is still damp. Two minutes, morning or evening, whichever fits your day — and the same steps every day matter more than doing them perfectly once.

Once a week, add the extra step for the rough spots: a little more moisturiser on the elbows, knees and shins after a warm shower. Change nothing else, and give the routine two quiet weeks before judging it. Skin that is washed gently and moisturised on damp skin every day stays soft, smooth and comfortable — and a routine this simple is one that lasts, because it never asks for more than a few calm minutes.
