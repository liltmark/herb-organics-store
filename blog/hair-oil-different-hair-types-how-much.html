Hair Oil for Different Hair Types: How Much Oil Your Hair Actually Needs

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 27 October 2026

Most hair oiling advice fails for one simple reason: it gives everyone the same amount. A palmful of oil might be exactly right for long, thick hair and far too much for fine, short hair. The result is predictable. One person oils their hair and loves how soft it feels, while another follows the same advice, ends up with heavy, flat hair that takes two washes to clean, and concludes that oiling simply does not suit them. Usually, oiling suits them perfectly well. The amount was wrong, not the habit. Getting the amount right for your hair type is the difference between oiling feeling like a treat and feeling like a chore.

Why the right amount matters so much

Hair oil works by coating the hair and scalp lightly, helping the hair feel smoother, softer and easier to comb. A light, even coating does that job well. A heavy coating does not do it better; it just sits on the surface, attracts dust, weighs the hair down, and forces you to wash harder to remove it. Washing harder then undoes some of the benefit of oiling in the first place. This is why more is not better with hair oil. The goal is the smallest amount that gives an even coating — no more, no less. When you find that amount, washing afterwards becomes easy, often with a single gentle wash, and the hair feels soft rather than heavy.

Fine hair: less than you think

Fine hair is the type most often over-oiled. Because each strand is thin, and because there is less hair overall to absorb the oil, even a modest amount can leave fine hair looking flat and greasy. If your hair is fine, start with a very small amount — roughly the size of a small coin warmed between your palms — and work it into the scalp first. Smooth only what remains on your hands down the lengths. Do not add a second helping unless the ends still feel dry after that first pass. In most cases, they will not need it.

Medium hair: the middle ground

Medium hair — neither very fine nor very thick, and usually of average density — is the most forgiving type when it comes to oiling. A coin-sized amount, or a little more for longer lengths, worked into the scalp and smoothed down the hair, is a good starting point. If your hair is shoulder-length, that amount will usually be enough. If it reaches past the shoulders, you may need a little extra for the lengths, added in a second small pass rather than all at once. Adding oil gradually is always easier than removing too much.

Thick, long or curly hair: more is genuinely needed

Thick hair, very long hair, and curly or wavy hair are the types that genuinely need more oil, and this is where generic advice often goes wrong in the opposite direction. There is simply more hair to cover, and curly hair in particular tends to feel drier along its lengths because its shape makes it harder for natural oils to travel down the strand. For this hair, a generous amount — two or three times what fine hair needs — is not excessive; it is what an even coating actually requires. Work in sections, as described in our weekly hair oiling routine, so that no part of the scalp or lengths is missed while another part is drenched.

Sectioning matters because thick and curly hair hides dry patches easily. Part the hair into four or six sections, oil the scalp in each section, and then smooth oil down each section in turn. This takes a few minutes longer, but it means you use oil where it is needed instead of piling it all on top. For very long hair, give the ends special attention: they have been around the longest, they have seen the most washing and brushing, and they will usually take — and benefit from — a little extra. Leaving the oil in longer also suits this hair type well; an hour, or overnight if you prefer, gives the oil time to spread and settle.

Short hair, and hair that is already oily

Hair that already feels oily at the roots needs a different approach rather than no oiling at all. Focus the oil on the lengths and ends, where it is most needed, and use only a very light touch on the scalp itself. You can also oil less often — once every ten days, for example — rather than every week. This way the drier lengths still get the conditioning they need, without adding weight where the hair is already heavy. If your scalp feels uncomfortable or oily soon after washing despite a light routine, it is worth checking whether hard water is part of the picture; our guide to hard water in Pakistan explains what to look for.

How to judge the right amount for you

Use the guides above as a starting point, not a final answer, and then let your own hair correct you. After oiling and washing, ask three simple questions. Did the hair feel soft and easy to comb? Did one gentle wash remove the oil comfortably? Did the hair still look light and natural the next day? If the answer to all three is yes, you have found your amount. If the hair still felt heavy after washing, use less next time. If the ends still felt dry and rough, use a little more on the lengths only, not on the scalp. Adjust one thing at a time, and give each change two or three weeks before judging it, because hair responds to a routine, not to a single application.
