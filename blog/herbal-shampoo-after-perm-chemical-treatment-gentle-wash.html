A Gentle Wash Routine for Hair After a Perm or Chemical Treatment

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 13 November 2026

What a perm or chemical treatment does to hair

A perm or chemical treatment changes the shape of the hair from the inside, and that change leaves the hair more delicate than usual for a while. The strands feel drier, they tangle more easily, and they lose moisture faster than untreated hair. The scalp can also feel more sensitive in the first days, and the hair may look frizzy or uneven while it settles into its new shape.

This is normal after a treatment, and it does not mean the hair is ruined. It means the hair needs a gentler routine while it adjusts. A harsh shampoo, very hot water, or rough handling will make the dryness worse, while a soft wash, lukewarm water, and a little patience will help the hair feel comfortable again. Our guide to caring for colour-treated hair with a gentle herbal wash follows the same idea: treated hair needs a softer touch, not a stronger product.

The first wash after a treatment

The first wash matters most, so take your time with it. Follow the timing your stylist advised before washing, since washing too soon can disturb the new shape while it is still setting. When the time comes, wet the hair with lukewarm water and let it soak for a minute, so the strands soften before any shampoo touches them.

Take a small amount of herbal shampoo, work it into a soft lather in the hands, and spread it gently over the scalp and through the lengths. Massage the scalp with the flat pads of the fingers, and let the lather run down the hair on its own. Rinse well and pat the hair dry with a soft towel. A gentle wash routine for treated hair

For the first few weeks after a treatment, keep every wash simple and slow. Wash with lukewarm water, never hot, and use only a small amount of shampoo. One gentle wash is enough: a second wash strips the moisture the hair is trying to hold, and it makes the frizz worse. Rinse with the water running straight down the hair, in the direction it grows, so the strands stay smooth and do not tangle.

After the wash, let the hair air dry until it is damp, then comb it through slowly with a wide-tooth comb, starting at the ends. If the hair feels dry at the ends, a drop of hair oil smoothed over the ends helps, but keep it light. Keeping treated hair soft between washes

Most of the care for treated hair happens between washes. The hair dries out faster than usual, so it needs a little extra moisture through the week. A light oiling once or twice a week, with a small amount of oil smoothed over the lengths and left to rest before washing, keeps the ends soft and easier to manage.

Sleep also matters for treated hair. A loose braid at night keeps the lengths from tangling while you sleep, and a soft pillowcase helps the hair slide instead of catching. During the day, keep the hair out of strong wind and harsh sun when you can, and avoid tight styles that pull on the roots. Our hair wash routine after oiling shows how to wash oil out gently, without stripping the hair, which suits treated hair well.

How often to wash treated hair

Treated hair usually does better with fewer washes. Two or three washes a week is a good starting point for most hair, with a gentle rinse on the days between if the scalp feels sweaty. Washing every day dries the hair out faster, and it makes the frizz and the tangles worse, not better.

Listen to the hair as the weeks pass. If the scalp feels itchy or the hair feels limp, the wash rhythm may need a small change, but change one thing at a time and give it a week before judging. In cold weather the hair may need even fewer washes, and our gentle winter wash routine for Pakistan shows how to adjust the wash when the air is cold and dry.

Mistakes to avoid after a chemical treatment

The first mistake is washing the hair too soon after the treatment, before the new shape has set. The second is using a harsh shampoo or very hot water, which strips the hair just when it needs moisture most. The third is brushing the hair hard while it is wet, which breaks the fragile strands at their weakest point.

The fourth mistake is piling on styling products to fight the frizz, which weighs the hair down and leaves it dull. And the fifth is expecting the hair to feel normal in a day: treated hair settles slowly, over a few weeks, and a steady, gentle routine is what brings it back to soft and manageable. Wash softly, handle the hair kindly, and give it time.
