Herbal Shampoo for Winter Hair: A Gentle Wash Routine for Dry, Cold-Weather Hair

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 14 November 2026

What winter does to hair

Cold air outside and warm, dry air inside leave hair drier in winter than in any other season. The hair loses moisture faster, the ends feel rough, and the scalp can feel tight or itchy. Hats and scarves add friction, and the hair can turn flat, full of static, or hard to manage by the afternoon.

The wash routine that works in summer often feels too harsh in winter. A wash that is too frequent, or water that is too hot, strips the little moisture the hair has left, and the hair comes out of the shower feeling straw-like. Winter hair needs a gentler wash, less often, with lukewarm water and a little extra care afterwards.

Washing less often in winter

In winter, most hair does well with two or three washes a week instead of a daily wash. The scalp produces less sweat in cold weather, and the hair stays cleaner for longer, so a daily wash is rarely needed. Washing less often lets the scalp keep its natural oils, which protect the hair from the dry winter air.

If the scalp feels oily between washes, a rinse with plain lukewarm water is enough to freshen the hair without a full wash. Our guide to frequent washing after gym and sweat explains when a wash is really needed and when a rinse will do, and the same idea applies in winter: wash when the hair needs it, not by the clock.

Lukewarm water, not hot

Hot water feels good on a cold morning, but it is hard on winter hair. It opens the hair cuticle and lets moisture escape, and it dries the scalp out, which can leave it tight and flaky. Lukewarm water cleans just as well and leaves the hair softer and easier to manage.

Keep the wash water lukewarm from start to finish, and finish with a cool rinse if you can manage it, since a cool rinse helps the hair lie flat and look shinier. The same lukewarm rule applies to the final rinse after any conditioner or oil treatment.

The gentle winter wash, step by step

Wet the hair well with lukewarm water, then take a small amount of Nabat herbal shampoo and work it into a soft lather in the hands before it touches the hair. Apply the lather to the scalp first, using the fingertips in slow circles, and let the lather run down the lengths as you rinse, instead of piling the hair up and scrubbing the ends.

Wash once, rinse well, and only wash a second time if the hair still feels oily or heavy. The lengths of the hair do not need direct scrubbing in winter; the lather that runs down them is enough to clean them, and it keeps the ends from drying out. Our wash routine for hair after swimming shows the same gentle order, with the scalp washed first and the lengths treated lightly.

Conditioning and oiling in winter

A little extra moisture makes the biggest difference to winter hair. After the wash, a light conditioner on the mid-lengths and ends helps the hair stay soft, and a small amount of hair oil on the ends before drying keeps them from splitting in the cold air. Our guide to hair oil in winter, cold wind and dry air shows how a light coat of oil protects the hair when the weather turns harsh.

If the scalp feels dry, a gentle scalp massage with a little warm oil before the wash helps too. Our scalp massage technique with hair oil explains the slow, light movements that work best, and a short massage once a week is enough in winter. Wash the oil out with the same gentle shampoo routine, and the scalp will feel calm rather than tight.

Drying hair in cold weather

Hair that is left wet in cold air takes a long time to dry, and it can feel cold and heavy all morning. Pat the hair gently with a soft towel to remove the water, instead of rubbing, since rubbing roughs the hair up and adds frizz. If you use a dryer, keep it on a low, warm setting and hold it a little further from the hair than usual.

Try not to go out with wet hair in cold weather, since the cold air makes wet hair stiff and hard to manage. Let the hair dry most of the way indoors, and cover it loosely with a scarf or a hat only once it is mostly dry, so the fabric does not rub the damp hair.

Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is washing winter hair every day with hot water, which leaves the hair dry and the scalp tight. The second is scrubbing the ends hard, which splits them faster in cold weather. The third is skipping conditioner or oil in winter, because the hair looks fine when it is wet and only turns rough once it dries.

The fourth mistake is going out with wet hair in the cold, which leaves it stiff and hard to comb. And the fifth is using the same summer routine all winter, without changing anything for the season. Wash less often, keep the water lukewarm, add a little moisture, and winter hair will stay soft and easy to manage.
