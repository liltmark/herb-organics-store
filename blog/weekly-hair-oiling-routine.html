The Weekly Hair Oiling Routine: How to Oil, Rest and Wash with Nabat Hair Oil

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 26 October 2026

Most of us grew up watching our mothers or grandmothers oil hair before wash day — and then, somewhere along the way, we stopped. Life got busy, wash-and-go took over, and oiling became something we meant to do but never quite got around to. Yet a simple weekly oiling routine is still one of the most practical things you can do for your hair: it takes about half an hour, it costs very little, and it makes wash day itself gentler. Here is a straightforward routine you can actually keep up, built around our Nabat Hair Oil.

Why weekly, not daily?

Daily oiling sounds dedicated, but in practice it leaves hair heavy, attracts dust, and forces you to wash more often — which defeats the purpose. Once a week is the sweet spot for most people: enough to keep the hair and scalp conditioned, but light enough that one good wash removes it cleanly. Pick a fixed day — the evening before your usual wash day works best — and treat it as part of the week's rhythm rather than a chore to fit in whenever. A routine you can repeat is worth far more than an ambitious one you abandon after two weeks.

The weekly routine, step by step

Set aside thirty minutes in the evening, and follow these steps:

  1. Warm the oil lightly. Pour a small amount of Nabat Hair Oil into a bowl and warm it briefly — a few seconds in warm water is enough. It should feel pleasantly warm on the wrist, never hot.
  2. Work in sections. Part your hair into four or six sections so you can reach the scalp evenly. Using your fingertips, apply the oil to the scalp section by section, then smooth what remains down the lengths. Do not drench the hair; a light, even coating is all you need.
  3. Massage gently. Spend five to ten minutes massaging the scalp with your fingertips in slow circles. Keep it comfortable and relaxed — this is not a workout, and pressing hard does not help. The massage also helps you notice dry or tight areas that need a little extra oil.
  4. Let it rest. Leave the oil in for at least thirty minutes, or overnight if you prefer, with a towel over your pillow. Longer is fine, but there is no need to leave it in for days — after a point, extra time adds little and only makes washing harder.
  5. Wash with a gentle shampoo. The next morning, wash with a modest amount of NABAT Herbal & Organic Shampoo, working it into the scalp and letting the lather run down the lengths. One thorough wash is usually enough; if the hair still feels oily, a second light lather on the scalp will finish the job.
  6. Dry gently. Pat the hair with a towel rather than rubbing vigorously, and let it air-dry when you can. Rough towel-drying is one of the easiest ways to create frizz and breakage after an otherwise perfect routine.

Mistakes that spoil a good oiling routine

Three mistakes come up again and again. The first is using far too much oil — a palmful is plenty for most hair, and excess oil just means extra washing. The second is scratching the scalp with nails during the massage, which can irritate the skin; fingertips only. The third is washing with a harsh shampoo straight after oiling, which strips away the conditioning you just gave your hair. A gentle, sulphate-free shampoo is the right partner for an oiling routine, because it cleans without undoing the work.

How much oil, and how often, for your hair?

Start with once a week and adjust from there. Very dry or long hair may enjoy twice a week in winter, while fine or naturally oily hair may prefer once every ten days. Watch how your hair feels after washing: if it feels soft and easy to comb, the amount and frequency are right; if it still feels heavy after washing, use a little less oil next time rather than washing harder. There is no single correct amount — the right routine is the one your hair responds to, kept up week after week.
