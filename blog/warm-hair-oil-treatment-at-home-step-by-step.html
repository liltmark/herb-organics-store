A Warm Oil Treatment at Home: A Step-by-Step Routine with Nabat Hair Oil

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 10 November 2026

What a warm oil treatment is

A warm oil treatment is a simple idea: the oil is warmed gently before it is applied, so it spreads more easily and sinks into the hair better than cold oil. In our homes, oiling has always been a regular habit, and warming the oil is the traditional way of making it feel more comfortable on the scalp, especially in winter. It is the same weekly oiling routine, with one extra step at the start.

Warm oil feels lighter and thinner than cold oil, so a small amount covers more hair and is easier to work through the lengths without pulling. The warmth also makes the scalp massage that follows feel more relaxing. This is a care routine, not a cure for any hair problem: it leaves the hair feeling softer and easier to manage, and turns a regular oiling session into a calm ritual. The whole treatment takes about an hour, and most of that time is simply resting.

Warming the oil safely

Pour the amount of oil you need into a small bowl — about two to three tablespoons for medium-length hair, a little more for long or thick hair. Never heat the oil directly over a flame or in a microwave: that can make it far too hot in seconds and spoil the oil. Instead, place the bowl in a larger bowl of hot water and leave it for two to three minutes. Stir once, and test a drop on the inside of your wrist. It should feel pleasantly warm, like warm water, never hot.

If the oil feels hot on your wrist, wait a minute and test again. Oil holds heat longer than water, so it cools more slowly than you expect. Only use oil that feels comfortable on the skin, and never apply oil that is steaming or too hot to touch: warm oil is soothing, hot oil burns. Once it is at the right temperature, use it straight away.

Applying section by section

Start with dry, detangled hair. Divide it into four sections — two at the front, two at the back — and clip each one out of the way. Take a little warm oil on your fingertips and work it into the scalp of the first section with slow, small circles, then move to the next section and repeat, until the whole scalp has a thin, even layer. Then smooth the remaining oil down the lengths, from the middle to the ends, where the hair is oldest and driest.

Sectioning is what makes the treatment even. Without it, the top layer gets all the oil and the hair underneath gets none, and the ends — the part that needs it most — are missed entirely. Use less oil than you think you need at first. Our pre-wash oiling guide for long hair shows the same sectioning method for a regular oiling session.

How long to rest, and covering the hair

Once the oil is applied, the hair needs time to rest. Thirty minutes is enough for a regular warm treatment, and an hour is better if you have the time. Cover the hair with a shower cap or a light cotton cloth while it rests: the cover keeps the warmth in, stops the oil from getting on clothes and furniture, and keeps the hair out of the way.

Use the resting time to relax: a cup of tea, a quiet half hour, and no rushing. Do not leave the oil on for a whole day, and do not sleep in it unless that is your normal routine. Our guide to overnight oiling versus short oiling explains how to choose between a long rest and a short one.

Washing the oil out properly

Washing warm oil out is the step that decides whether the treatment feels like a success. Wet the hair fully with lukewarm water first, then apply a small amount of gentle shampoo to the scalp and work it in with your fingertips, section by section. The shampoo needs to reach the scalp through the oil, so take your time. Rinse well, and wash a second time with half the shampoo if the hair still feels oily.

Two washes are normal after an oil treatment — the first lifts most of the oil, and the second finishes the job. What matters is that both washes are gentle and the rinsing is thorough. If the hair still feels heavy after two washes, the problem is usually too much oil at the start, not too little shampoo, so use a little less oil next time rather than a harsher wash. Our weekly hair oiling routine shows the same wash-out method, and our scalp massage technique explains the gentle fingertip circles that work best during the wash as well as the oiling.

Mistakes to avoid with a warm oil treatment

The first mistake is heating the oil too much. Oil that is too hot can burn the scalp, and it can also spoil the oil itself. Always warm it in a bowl of hot water, always test it on your wrist, and never apply oil that feels hot. The second is using too much oil: warm oil spreads further than cold oil, so the usual amount goes further, and too much only makes the wash-out harder.

The third mistake is rushing the rest: a warm treatment needs at least thirty minutes to feel worthwhile, and washing the oil straight out again wastes the whole point of warming it. And the fourth is washing it out with very hot water or a harsh shampoo, which strips the hair of the very softness the treatment was meant to give. Keep the oil warm, not hot, the application even, the rest unhurried, and the wash gentle.
