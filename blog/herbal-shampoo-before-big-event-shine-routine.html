Washing Hair Before a Big Event: A Gentle Routine for Shine and Softness

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 9 November 2026

Plan the wash a day ahead

Good hair on the day of a big event starts the day before. The biggest mistake people make is trying a new product, a new style or a new wash routine on the morning of the event itself. Hair that is used to a gentle routine reacts best to that same routine, done with a little more care. Plan the wash for the day before the event, or for the morning if your hair holds a style well, and change nothing else.

A wash the day before gives the hair time to settle. Freshly washed hair can feel too soft and slippery to hold a style, while hair washed the day before has a little natural grip and still looks clean and shiny. If your hair gets oily quickly, wash it on the morning of the event instead, but use the same gentle routine you always use. The goal is hair that looks like your hair on its best day, not hair that has been through an experiment.

Start with a gentle pre-wash

Before the water touches your hair, brush it gently from the ends upward, working out any tangles a little at a time. Brushing dry hair before washing spreads the natural oils along the strands and keeps the wash from turning small tangles into knots. Use a wide-tooth comb or a soft brush, and never pull through a knot from the top — start below it and work up slowly.

Then wet the hair fully with lukewarm water. Hot water feels good, but it lifts the outer layer of the hair and leaves it looking dull, while very cold water makes it harder for the shampoo to spread. Lukewarm water is the middle ground that cleans well and keeps the shine. Let the water run through the hair for a good minute so every strand is wet before the shampoo goes on.

The wash itself: two light lathers

Take a small amount of herbal shampoo — about a coin-sized drop for shoulder-length hair — and work it into a lather in your hands before it touches your hair. Massage it into the scalp with your fingertips, not your nails, in slow circles. The scalp is where the oil and sweat gather, so that is where the shampoo belongs. The lengths of the hair do not need to be scrubbed; the lather running down them as you rinse is enough.

Rinse well, then lather a second time with an even smaller amount. The first wash lifts the oil and dust away, and the second wash cleans the scalp properly with very little shampoo. This is the same two-wash rhythm we recommend in our hair wash routine after oiling: the first wash does the heavy lifting, the second finishes the job gently. Rinse until the water runs clear and the hair feels clean but not squeaky.

Drying for shine and softness

How you dry your hair matters as much as how you wash it. Squeeze the water out gently with your hands first, then wrap the hair in a soft towel and press — never rub, because rubbing roughs up the hair surface and creates frizz. Let the towel take the worst of the water, then let the hair air-dry as much as you can before any styling.

If you need heat, keep it low and keep the dryer moving, holding it at least a handspan away from the hair. A little patience here pays off: hair that dries slowly and calmly looks shinier than hair that has been blasted dry in a hurry. For extra protection before heat, a light coat of oil on the lengths helps, and our pre-styling hair oil routine shows how little you need.

Softness without heavy products

After the hair is dry, a tiny amount of hair oil on the ends adds softness and shine without weighing the hair down. Warm two or three drops between your palms, smooth them over the ends only, and leave the roots alone. Oily roots on the day of an event are the one thing that can undo a good wash, so keep every product away from the scalp once the hair is dry.

If your hair is curly, the same gentle wash works well — our curly hair wash routine keeps curls soft without heavy build-up. And if your hair is coloured, the gentle wash matters even more, because harsh washing fades colour fast; our colour-treated hair routine shows how to keep colour looking fresh longer. The rule is the same for every hair type: wash gently, use little product, and let the hair settle.

What to avoid before the event

Do not try a new shampoo, oil or styling product in the days before the event. A product your hair has never met can leave it limp, greasy or frizzy, and there is no time to fix it. Do not oil the hair heavily the night before either — a heavy oiling needs a thorough wash to remove, and the morning wash may not be enough. If you want to oil, do it a few days earlier, not the night before.

Avoid very hot water, tight styles that pull at the roots, and sleeping on wet hair, which leaves it creased and frizzy by morning. Wash with the routine your hair already knows, dry it calmly, add a drop of oil to the ends, and leave it alone. Planned a day ahead and done gently, the wash gives you hair that is clean, soft and shiny — and one less thing to worry about on the big day.
