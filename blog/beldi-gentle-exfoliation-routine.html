A Gentle Exfoliation Routine with Beldi Black Soap: How Often and How to Do It

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 29 October 2026

Exfoliation sounds like a big word for a simple idea. It just means helping the surface of your skin shed the dull, dry layer that builds up over time, so the skin underneath feels smoother and looks fresher. Done gently, it can turn an ordinary shower into a small weekly ritual. Done too hard or too often, it leaves skin feeling tight, red, and uncomfortable. The difference is almost always in the method, not the product. Beldi black soap fits this ritual well because it was made for it: in the Moroccan hammam, the soft olive-based soap is smoothed over warm skin, left to soften, and then followed by a light pass with a mitt. This guide shows how to bring that same calm approach home, without scrubbing hard or rushing the steps.

What gentle exfoliation actually means

Gentle exfoliation is not about force. It is about softening the skin first, then using light, even movements to lift away surface build-up. Warm water and a few minutes of steam from a shower do most of the softening work for you. The soap then helps the surface feel clean and smooth as you massage it over the skin, and a soft mitt or cloth provides the light friction that finishes the job. If your skin feels sore, red, or tight afterwards, the routine was too strong for that day, not more effective. The goal is skin that feels clean, calm, and comfortable when you step out of the shower.

How often should you exfoliate?

For most people, once a week is a sensible starting point. That gives the skin time to settle between sessions and makes the routine easy to remember, for example as a relaxed weekend wash. People with oilier skin, or those who spend a lot of time outdoors in dust and heat, may be comfortable with twice a week, while people with dry or sensitive skin may prefer once every ten days or so. The right frequency is the one your skin tolerates happily: smooth and comfortable afterwards, with no lingering redness or dryness the next day.

The routine, step by step

Start with a warm shower, long enough for the skin to feel soft and damp all over, usually five to ten minutes. Warm water is enough; very hot water can leave the skin feeling dry, which defeats the purpose of a gentle routine. Scoop a small amount of Beldi black soap and smooth it over one area at a time, starting with the arms and legs and then moving to the back and shoulders. Let it sit on the skin for a couple of minutes while you continue washing elsewhere, so the soap has time to soften the surface.

Next, take a soft exfoliating mitt or a damp washcloth and move it over the skin in small, light circles. Keep the pressure gentle and even, and spend a little longer on areas that often feel rough, such as elbows, knees, and heels. Avoid broken skin, sunburn, or any area that is already irritated, and be extra light on the chest and neck. Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water until the skin feels clean but not squeaky, and pat dry with a soft towel instead of rubbing hard. The whole routine takes little more than your usual shower, just done at a calmer pace with a bit more attention.

Skin care around the routine

What you do before and after matters as much as the exfoliation itself. Beforehand, keep things simple: a normal warm shower and clean skin are all the preparation needed, and there is no need for extra products on exfoliation day. Afterwards, while the skin is still slightly damp, apply your usual moisturiser or body oil, since damp skin tends to absorb it more comfortably. If you are going out in the sun afterwards, take your usual sun care, because freshly exfoliated skin can be more sensitive to strong sunlight. Signs to slow down

Even a gentle routine can be too much on a given week, and the skin will usually say so plainly. Lingering redness, a stinging feeling when you apply moisturiser, flaking, or skin that feels tight for hours afterwards are all signs to ease off. When that happens, skip exfoliation the following week, return to a plain, gentle wash, and let the skin settle before trying again with lighter pressure or a longer gap between sessions. A routine that bends with the condition of your skin will serve you far better than one followed rigidly every week regardless of how the skin feels.

Fitting it into your week

The easiest way to keep this routine going is to tie it to a day you already wash slowly, such as a Friday evening or a weekend morning, when there is no rush. Keep the mitt and the soap together in the bathroom, keep the soap container dry between uses so it stays in good condition, and think of the session as a small ritual rather than a chore.
