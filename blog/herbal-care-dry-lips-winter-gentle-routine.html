Herbal Care for Dry Lips in Winter: A Gentle Routine for Soft Lips

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 10 November 2026

Why lips dry out in winter

Lips dry out faster than any other skin in winter, and there is a simple reason for it: the skin on the lips is thinner than the skin anywhere else on the face, and it has no oil glands of its own. The rest of the face makes a little natural oil to protect itself, but the lips cannot. In winter, cold air outside and dry, heated air inside pull moisture away from them all day, and there is nothing to replace it. The result is familiar: dry, tight lips that crack at the corners and feel rough when you smile.

Licking the lips makes it worse, even though it feels like it helps. Saliva dries quickly in cold air and leaves the lips drier than before, and repeated licking can leave the skin around the lips red and sore. The fix is a short, gentle routine: keep the lips clean, soften them with warm water, smooth on a thin layer of oil to hold the moisture in, and repeat it through the day. A small amount of plain herbal oil does this job well, because it sits on the surface and slows down the moisture loss.

Start with clean hands and a gentle wash

Everything that touches the lips should be clean, and that starts with the hands. Wash your hands first with a gentle soap and lukewarm water, and dry them well. Then wash the lips themselves, very gently, with lukewarm water and a tiny amount of mild soap on a fingertip — or with water alone if the lips are already sore. The point is to remove the dust, food and dried saliva that sit on the lips through the day, so the oil goes onto clean skin.

Be gentle here. The lips are thin skin, and scrubbing them with a rough cloth or a brush only makes them sorer. One soft pass with a wet fingertip is enough. If the lips have flaky skin on them, do not pick or peel it off — that tears the skin underneath and leaves it raw. Our winter hand care routine follows the same order — clean hands first, gentle wash, then moisture — and the lips need that same care, just smaller and softer.

Soften first with warm water

Before any oil goes on, the lips need a little moisture to hold in. Press a small piece of clean cloth dipped in warm water against the lips for about thirty seconds. The warmth softens the surface, loosens any dry flakes, and leaves the lips slightly damp — the same idea as putting moisturiser on damp skin after a wash: the moisture goes in first, and the oil then keeps it there.

Do not use hot water: on thin lip skin it stings and dries it out further. Warm water — the temperature you would comfortably wash your face with — is right. If the lips are cracked and sore, skip the cloth and simply let lukewarm water run over them briefly, then pat them gently with a soft towel.

A thin layer of oil, smoothed on gently

Take one small drop of plain herbal oil on a clean fingertip and smooth it over the lips in one gentle pass. A thin layer is enough — too much oil feels heavy, slides off, and ends up on everything you touch. Press the lips together lightly once to spread it evenly, and leave it alone. The oil sits on the surface and slows down the moisture loss, which is exactly what thin lip skin needs in winter.

Reapply through the day, whenever the lips start to feel dry again — after eating, after drinking tea, and before going out into the cold. A small bottle in a pocket or a bag makes this easy. At night, apply a slightly thicker layer before bed, so the lips have hours of protection while you sleep. Our gentle routine for dry elbows and knees uses the same idea on rougher skin, and our neck and chest care routine shows how a thin layer of oil protects skin that is often forgotten.

Protecting lips through the day

The routine works best when it is repeated, not when it is done once. Lips lose moisture all day in winter, so a single morning application will not last until the evening. The habit to build is simple: oil after every meal, oil before going outside, and oil before bed. Three or four light applications a day keep the lips comfortable, and each takes only a few seconds.

Covering the lips helps too. A scarf pulled gently over the mouth on a cold morning keeps the wind off the lips, and drinking water regularly through the day helps the skin from the inside. These small habits add up: protected lips crack less, heal faster, and stay soft through the whole winter. Our daily routine for arms and legs is built on the same idea — a short daily habit, repeated without fuss, keeps the skin soft.

Habits that make dry lips worse

The first habit to drop is licking the lips. It feels like relief for a few seconds, but saliva dries fast in cold air and leaves the lips drier than before. The second is picking at dry flakes. Pulling a flake off tears the skin underneath and leaves a raw spot that takes days to heal. Let warm water and oil soften the flakes instead.

The third is using a strongly flavoured or heavily scented lip product. Anything that stings, tingles or smells strong can irritate thin lip skin, especially when it is already cracked. Plain oil, with no added fragrance, is the safest choice. And the fourth is forgetting the lips entirely until they hurt.
