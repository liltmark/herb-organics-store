Hand and Foot Care with Beldi Black Soap: A Simple Routine for Softer Hands and Feet

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 31 October 2026

Hands and feet do more work than any other part of the body, and it shows. Hands are washed again and again through the day, handled with soap, dishwater and dust, while feet spend hours inside closed shoes, socks and sandals. Both end up dry, rough or tired-looking far sooner than the rest of the skin, yet both are usually rushed through in a shower, with whatever soap is nearest and no real routine at all. A short, separate hand and foot care routine with Beldi black soap changes that. It takes only a few extra minutes, a couple of times a week, and it gives the parts of the body that work hardest a little of the same gentle attention the face usually gets.

Why hands and feet need their own routine

The skin on the palms and soles is thicker than the skin elsewhere, which is why it can feel rough even when the rest of the body feels fine. At the same time, the backs of the hands and the tops of the feet are thin and exposed, so they dry out quickly in cold air, hot water and harsh soaps. A single quick wash cannot deal with both problems at once. Hands need gentle, frequent care that does not strip them further, while feet benefit from a short soak that softens hard skin before anything else is done. Treating them as one quick job is why so many people find their hands still feel dry and their heels still feel rough, even though they wash every day.

Step one: a short warm soak

Start with a basin or tub of warm water, never hot, and soak the feet for five to ten minutes while you wash the hands separately. The soak softens rough skin on the heels and soles, loosens everyday dust and makes the soap work far better afterwards. While the feet soak, wet the hands, take a pea-sized amount of Beldi black soap and work it into a smooth paste between damp palms. Wash the backs of the hands, between the fingers and around the nails with slow, gentle circles, then rinse with lukewarm water. Do not use very hot water for either step, because hot water leaves skin feeling tighter and drier once it cools, which defeats the purpose of the routine.

Step two: wash the feet, section by section

Once the feet have soaked, lift one foot out at a time and apply a small amount of Beldi black soap, again warmed into a paste first. Work over the top of the foot, between the toes, around the ankle and across the sole, using the palms and fingers rather than a brush. Give the heels and the balls of the feet a little extra attention with slow circles, but keep the pressure light and even. These are the areas that feel roughest, yet pressing hard or scrubbing does not soften them faster; it only leaves the skin feeling sore. Rinse each foot well before moving to the other, and check between the toes, where soap residue is easy to miss and can leave the skin feeling itchy.

Step three: the mitt, used lightly and only where needed

After washing, a soft bath mitt or gentle exfoliating glove can be used on the feet, and only very lightly on the hands if the skin feels rough. Work over the heels and soles with short, even strokes, and skip any area that feels sensitive, cracked or sore. The mitt helps lift away the dull surface layer that builds up on feet, which is why they feel smoother afterwards, but one gentle pass is enough. Hands rarely need the mitt at all, and the backs of the hands should never be scrubbed, since that skin is thin and marks easily. If the skin ever feels red or tender after the mitt, leave it out for the next few sessions and let the skin settle before trying again.

Step four: moisturise, and give it time to sink in

Pat the hands and feet dry with a soft towel instead of rubbing, and leave the skin slightly damp. Then apply your usual moisturiser or body oil, starting with the driest areas: the knuckles and cuticles on the hands, and the heels and soles on the feet. Rub it in slowly until it sinks in, rather than leaving a thick layer sitting on the surface. For feet, this is the moment that makes the biggest difference. Put on clean cotton socks straight after moisturising and keep them on for an hour, or overnight if you are doing the routine in the evening. The socks hold the moisture close to the skin and stop it rubbing off on bedding or floors, so the feet feel noticeably softer by morning.

How often, and what to avoid

For most people, this full hand and foot routine twice a week is plenty, with plain gentle washing on the other days. Doing the mitt step every day can overdo it, leaving skin sensitive rather than smooth, so treat the soak and mitt as a twice-weekly ritual rather than a daily chore. Avoid sharing mitts or basins between people, avoid soaking feet that have cuts or sores, and avoid very hot water or harsh scrubbing at all times.
