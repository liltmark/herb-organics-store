Moroccan Beldi Black Soap: Benefits, Hammam Ritual & How to Use It

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 21 October 2026

In Morocco, the weekly hammam is more than a bath — it is a ritual of deep rest and renewal that has been practised for centuries. And at the heart of that ritual sits a small, unassuming jar: Beldi Black Soap. It does not look like the soap bars most of us grew up with, and it is not used like one either. If you have ever wondered what Beldi soap actually is, what it does for skin, and how the hammam ritual works, this guide walks you through all of it — including how to recreate the experience in your own bathroom in Pakistan.

What is Beldi Black Soap?

Beldi is a traditional Moroccan soap with a soft, rich, paste-like texture — closer to a thick gel or butter than a hard bar. It is made from olives: olive oil and crushed olives are saponified (turned into soap) to create the dark, glossy paste, which carries the natural goodness and minerals of the olive itself. Because it is a paste, a little spreads a long way across damp skin. Beldi has been treasured for generations as "the ancient secret to naturally soft, clean and glowing skin" — not as a daily soap, but as a weekly treatment that prepares the skin for deep exfoliation.

The benefits of Beldi Black Soap

Used the traditional way, Beldi offers a combination of cleansing and care that ordinary soap rarely matches:

  • Deep cleanses. It removes dirt, excess oil and the day's impurities thoroughly, leaving skin feeling genuinely clean rather than coated.
  • Gently exfoliates. The paste softens the surface of the skin so dead skin cells can be lifted away with a mitt, helping skin feel smoother and softer.
  • Unclogs pores. By clearing away buildup, it helps keep pores from becoming blocked and keeps skin feeling fresh.
  • Nourishes naturally. Being packed with olive goodness and natural minerals, it cleans without the tight, stripped feeling many harsh soaps leave behind.
  • Perfect for hammam. It is the traditional first step of the hammam ritual — the treatment that makes the exfoliation that follows so effective.

The hammam ritual, step by step

You do not need a Moroccan bathhouse to enjoy Beldi — a warm shower and a kessa mitt (the traditional exfoliating glove) are enough. Here is the ritual:

  1. Warm your skin. Take a warm shower or bath for several minutes. The warmth and steam soften your skin and open the way for deep cleansing.
  2. Apply the paste. While your skin is still damp, spread a thin layer of Beldi Black Soap over your body — and on the face too, with caution, avoiding the eye area.
  3. Leave it for a few minutes. Let the soap sit so it can soften dead skin and loosen buildup. Relax — this pause is part of the ritual.
  4. Scrub with the kessa mitt. Using gentle, firm strokes, scrub your skin with the exfoliating mitt. You may see softened dead skin roll away — that is the exfoliation working.
  5. Rinse thoroughly. Wash everything off with warm water and pat your skin dry.
  6. Moisturise. Follow with your usual moisturiser or body oil while skin is still slightly damp.

Beldi suits the whole body — body, hands and feet, and the face when used carefully. Because it is a treatment rather than a daily soap, use it 1–2 times per week. If your skin is very sensitive, try it on a small area first, keep face use gentle and brief, and skip it on broken or irritated skin. One weekly ritual is all it takes to keep skin feeling smooth, clean and refreshed.
