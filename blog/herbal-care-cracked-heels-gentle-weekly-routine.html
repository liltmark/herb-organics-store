Herbal Care for Cracked Heels: A Gentle Weekly Routine

Skin Care By Herb & Organics Co. 11 November 2026

Why heels crack and dry out

Heels take a lot of wear. They carry the whole body with every step, they press against hard floors and open shoes, and the skin there is thicker and drier than almost anywhere else on the body. In dry weather, or after weeks of walking barefoot or in open sandals, that thick skin loses moisture, turns rough, and then splits into small cracks. The cracks are uncomfortable, and they catch on socks and sheets.

Cracked heels are not a sign of poor care, and they are very common. The skin on the heels simply needs more help than the skin elsewhere: regular washing, gentle smoothing, and a good layer of moisture. A weekly routine, done patiently, keeps the heels soft and stops small cracks from becoming deep ones. If a crack is deep, bleeding, or very sore, see a doctor rather than trying to fix it at home.

The weekly heel routine at a glance

The routine has three steps and takes about twenty minutes, once a week. First, a warm soak and a gentle wash, which softens the hard skin and cleans the feet. Second, gentle smoothing, which takes away the loose, rough surface. Third, oil and moisture, sealed in with socks, which lets the heels drink the moisture in overnight. Between weekly sessions, a quick daily habit of moisturiser and socks keeps the progress from slipping back.

Pick a regular evening for the routine, so it becomes a habit rather than a chore. After a bath or a shower is a good time, since the feet are already warm and the skin is soft. Step one: a warm soak and gentle wash

Fill a basin with warm water, not hot, and soak the feet for ten minutes. The warm water softens the thick skin on the heels and loosens the dry, flaky surface. Then wash the feet gently with a small amount of Beldi black soap, working it into a soft lather over the whole foot, with attention to the heels and the edges of the feet, where cracks start. Keep the wash short and gentle, and rinse well.

Hot water and harsh scrubbing do more harm than good here. Very hot water dries the heels out further, and scrubbing hard only roughens the skin. The soak does the softening, and the soap does the cleaning. Pat the feet dry with a soft towel, and do not rub: the skin is soft after the soak, and rubbing can tear it. Step two: smoothing rough skin

While the heels are still slightly damp, smooth the rough skin gently with a foot file or a pumice stone. Use light, even strokes in one direction, and stop as soon as the surface feels smoother. The aim is to take away the loose, flaky layer, not to file the heel down in one session. A little smoothing each week is far better than one hard session.

Rinse the feet again after smoothing, and pat dry. If the heels feel tender after smoothing, you have done too much, so ease off next week. Never use a blade or a sharp tool on the heels: cutting the skin is dangerous. The file and the soak together are enough, and the moisture step that follows does the real work of softening the heels.

Step three: oil, moisture and socks

Now smooth a generous layer of plain moisturiser over the heels, or a thin layer of hair oil if that is what you have at hand. Massage it in slowly, so the skin takes it in, and cover the whole heel and the edges of the foot. Then put on a pair of clean cotton socks and leave them on overnight. The socks hold the moisture against the skin while you sleep, and by morning the heels feel noticeably softer.

This overnight step is the part that makes the biggest difference, and it is also the part most often skipped. Moisturiser rubbed on and left open to the air dries away quickly, while the same moisturiser under socks works all night. Daily habits that keep heels soft

The weekly routine works best when the daily habits support it. Smooth on a little moisturiser after every bath or shower, while the skin is still slightly damp, so the heels never dry out completely between sessions. Wear closed shoes or socks around the house instead of walking barefoot on hard floors, which dries the heels and presses them flat.

Check the heels once a week, at the same time as the routine, so small cracks are caught early, when a little extra moisture is all they need. If the heels stay soft through the week, the routine is working. If they crack again quickly, add a second short session mid-week: a warm soak, a gentle wash, and moisturiser with socks.
