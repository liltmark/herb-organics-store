Herbal Shampoo After Swimming: A Gentle Wash for Chlorine and Salt Water

Hair Care By Herb & Organics Co. 11 November 2026

What chlorine and salt water leave in hair

Swimming is good exercise, but pool and sea water are hard on hair. Chlorine is added to pools to keep the water clean, and it stays in the hair after you get out, drying it and leaving it rough and tangled. Salt water does much the same: the salt draws moisture out of the hair as it dries, and the hair can feel stiff and hard to comb. If the hair is left unwashed after a swim, the chlorine and salt keep working on it for hours.

That is why the wash after swimming matters more than the wash on an ordinary day. The job is simple: get the chlorine and salt out of the hair, gently, before they dry in. A harsh shampoo used twice will strip the hair and leave it worse, so the after-swim wash should be one soft wash with a mild shampoo, done soon after the swim.

Rinse first, before any shampoo

The most useful step after a swim costs nothing: a long rinse with plain water. Stand under a shower or a tap and let clean water run through the hair for a full minute or two before you reach for the shampoo. Water alone carries away most of the chlorine and salt, and it softens the hair so the shampoo can work on a clean surface instead of fighting through a coating of chemicals.

This rinse also helps if you swim often. Hair that is rinsed well after every swim needs less shampoo, and less shampoo means less stripping over the week. The after-swim wash, step by step

After the rinse, take a small amount of herbal shampoo and work it into a soft lather in your hands. Spread it through the hair from the scalp to the ends, and massage the scalp gently with the flat pads of your fingers. Keep the wash to one round: thirty seconds of gentle lather is enough to lift out what the rinse left behind, and a second wash would only dry the hair further.

Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water until the hair feels clean and smooth, not slippery. If the hair still smells faintly of chlorine, that is normal after a long pool session, and it fades as the hair dries. Do not add more shampoo to chase the smell away. How much washing is enough

If you swim once or twice a week, one gentle wash after each swim is enough, and your normal wash routine can carry the rest of the week. If you swim almost every day, the hair is being washed far more than usual, so keep each wash as light as possible: a good rinse, a small amount of shampoo, and one round of lather. On days when you only dip in briefly, a long rinse with plain water and no shampoo at all is often enough.

The sign to watch is how the hair feels when it is dry. Soft, easy-to-comb hair means the routine is right. Hair that feels rough or tangled after a few weeks of swimming needs more care, not more shampoo: a longer rinse, a lighter wash, and a little oil on the lengths after washing. After the wash: drying and a light oil finish

After the wash, the hair is at its most fragile, so treat it gently. Pat it dry instead of rubbing, and comb it with a wide-tooth comb starting from the ends, working up slowly. Rough combing on wet, swim-washed hair is one of the main causes of breakage, and it is easy to avoid by taking an extra minute.

Once the hair is mostly dry, a very small amount of hair oil smoothed over the lengths keeps the ends soft and protects them until the next swim. Keep the oil off the scalp, where it is not needed after a wash, and use only a drop or two: the aim is softness, not a heavy coating. Mistakes to avoid after swimming

The first mistake is skipping the rinse and the wash and letting the hair dry with chlorine or salt still in it. The chemicals keep drying the hair long after the swim is over. The second is washing twice with a strong shampoo, which strips the hair and leaves it rougher than one gentle wash would. The third is using very hot water, which opens the hair up and lets more chlorine in.

The fourth mistake is brushing or combing wet hair hard, especially from the roots down, which snaps the hair at its weakest point. And the fifth is forgetting the scalp: chlorine can leave the scalp dry and itchy too, so the wash should reach the scalp gently, not just the lengths.
